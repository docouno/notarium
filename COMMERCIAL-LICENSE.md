# Commercial licensing

Notarium is free and open source software, licensed under the
[GNU Affero General Public License, version 3](LICENSE) (`AGPL-3.0-only`).
**Every feature is open** — there is no paywalled edition, no crippled
"community" build, and no feature held back for paying customers. One codebase,
fully open, for everyone.

For most people the AGPL-3.0 is all you ever need, at no cost.

## When the AGPL-3.0 is enough (free)

You can use, run, self-host, study, and modify Notarium for free under the
AGPL-3.0 — including inside a company as an internal tool for your own work.
The only condition the AGPL adds over a permissive license is reciprocity: if
you modify Notarium and let others interact with it **over a network**, you must
make your modified source available to those users under the same license
(AGPL-3.0, section 13).

Examples that are fine under the AGPL, for free:

- An individual or a team self-hosting Notarium for their own notes.
- A company running Notarium internally as a knowledge base for its staff.
- Contributing changes back, or running a modified fork whose source you share
  with its users as the AGPL requires.

## When you need a commercial license (paid)

Buy a commercial license if you want to build a business **on Notarium itself
as a product** and the AGPL-3.0's copyleft obligations do not work for you —
typically because you cannot or will not release your derivative work's source
under the AGPL. This covers, for example:

- **Reselling** Notarium, or a derivative of it, as your own product.
- Offering Notarium as a **hosted / managed service (SaaS)** to third parties.
- **Embedding** Notarium in a proprietary product you distribute or sell.
- Running a **competing commercial service** built on Notarium without
  complying with the AGPL's source-disclosure terms.

A commercial license lifts the AGPL-3.0 copyleft obligations for your use under
separately negotiated terms. Using Notarium *inside* your own organization as a
tool (your business being about something else) does **not** require one — that
is covered by the AGPL above.
