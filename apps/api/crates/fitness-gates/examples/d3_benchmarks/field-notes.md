## Morning walk

We walked the short path from the gate to the stone wall before the first visitors arrived. The east bed was still dark from overnight rain. The old pear tree has kept its canopy open above the bench, so the early light falls straight across the gravel.

The notebook stays with the field kit. Each visit adds a small observation; larger changes belong in the seasonal plan with a date and a reason. This makes it easier to compare the actual work with the drawing on the potting shed door.

## Working observations

- The gravel holds less water near the bottom step than it did last spring.
- The first row of seedlings is easier to follow after the white markers were moved.
- Our rain gauge needs a clearer scale near the lip. Keep the old readings until the replacement arrives.
- Two benches are in shade at noon. The one nearest the apple trees has the best view of the beds.

## Water and light

The shallow beds by the south wall dry at different rates even when they receive the same amount of rain. A single afternoon reading rarely says much. Record the morning condition and the late reading on the same page. We can compare the pairs after the weather settles.

The tall hedge shields the middle section from wind. Leaves gather at the turn in the path and sometimes cover the drain. A quick check at the end of the afternoon is enough to keep the channel clear.

## Visit record

The volunteer picking up the afternoon shift should be able to find the unfinished work in this notebook. Record what changed, where it happened, and what you would like the next person to check. A short entry is usually easier to revisit than a photograph without a caption.

We kept the planting plan on paper for the first part of the season. Wind and rain made it hard to annotate on site, so we now take short field notes and transfer the measured distances at the end of each visit. Keep the original measurements in the notebook even if the proper drawing uses rounded values.

## Observations at the far gate

At the far gate the path divides around the largest tree. The northern fork stays cool and the ground feels soft after rain. A small board by the fence carries a map for visitors. We checked that the arrows still agree with the view from the entrance and that the letters can be read from the gravel.

The last row of labels has faded in direct light. The names are recorded in the ledger beside the tool cupboard. Replace the paper labels in batches, then compare the names with the copy kept in the shed.
